# Electrical and control schematic standards: research for a VizCraft notation pack

Scope: electrical and control schematic standards only. Sources marked (weak) are vendor blogs or forums. Paid standards were not opened; edition data come from publishers' catalogue pages. Artwork must be drawn from the public descriptions below, never traced from IEC 60617 images, IEEE 315 figures or another tool's library. The search budget ran out before IEC 60204-1 edition data and the NEMA ICS 19 symbol table could be confirmed; those gaps are flagged.

IEEE 945 (preferred metric units, now 945-2019) concerns units, not symbols, and is irrelevant ([IEEE](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/945/1312/)).

Style differences (secondary sources): ANSI resistor is a zigzag, IEC a rectangle often holding the value; ANSI ground is three shortening bars, IEC sources disagree between bars and an open triangle, so draw earth per 60617 wording; switch and contact hinge and gap details differ ([JLCPCB](https://jlcpcb.com/blog/circuit-symbols-electrical-electronic-diagrams), [kth-electric](https://kth-electric.com/en/electrical-symbols-guide-iec-ansi/), both weak). IEC 60617-12 calls distinctive gate shapes "non-preferred" without prohibiting them; IEEE 91 says they are not in contradiction ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logic_gate)). JIC contacts (two short bars, diagonal for NC) and circle coils contrast with IEC hinged-line contacts and rectangle coils (section 3).

Tool switching: Autodesk AutoCAD Electrical ships JIC/NFPA, IEC Legacy (60617 glyphs, one-letter 61346 tags) and IEC 60617 (same glyphs, two-letter 81346 tags) libraries, assigned per project; an NFPA icon menu can be mixed with an IEC library, drawing-resident blocks override the new library until purged, and scale differs by 25.4 between inch and metric sheets ([Autodesk forum](https://forums.autodesk.com/t5/autocad-electrical-forum/change-jic-to-iec/td-p/6459748), [Autodesk forum](https://forums.autodesk.com/t5/autocad-electrical-forum/iec-symbols-add-in/m-p/3593874)). Lesson: glyph style is a per-document variant on shared element types, and tag format is a separate setting.

Ladder conventions ([EC&M](https://www.ecmweb.com/basics/article/20897064/electrical-ladder-drawing-basics), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladder_logic), [industrialmonitordirect (weak)](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/control-panel-wiring-diagram-standards-wire-numbering-component-labels-and-drawing-conventions)):

- Vertical rails L1 and L2 (often heavier) carry control voltage; the power portion (disconnect, fuses, starter, overloads, motor) is drawn separately.
- Rungs numbered down the left rail; NC stops in series then NO starts in parallel on the left, one output on the right; outputs only in parallel.
- Numbers right of a coil list the rungs holding its contacts; underlined means NC.
- Wire numbers change at every device and are shared on a node; page/line schemes (2003 = page 2 line 3) with suffix letters are common, project standards govern.
- Rails carry continuation arrows between sheets; NO/NC state is read from the symbol, devices drawn de-energised.
- Appearance (secondary sources): JIC NO contact is two short bars, NC adds a diagonal; coil is a circle with the tag inside; overload is a heater loop with paired NC; pilot light a circle with rays and colour letter; fuse a lozenge; disconnect a hinged blade. IEC draws contacts as a hinged line (gap for NO), coils as rectangles with A1/A2, overload as a rectangle with the thermal qualifier, lamps as a crossed circle.

## 6. Machine-checkable validation rules by document type

- Circuit diagram (IEC or ladder): unique reference designation per device within its aspect tree; every connection has a wire number or potential name, changing across every device and identical on a node; every contact's parent coil exists and the coil's mirror lists every contact; no two coils share a designation; interrupted lines paired exactly once with matching page/zone at both ends; cross-references resolve; terminal numbers unique and typed (13/14 NO, 21/22 NC, A1/A2 coil); one output per rung; drawn contacts do not exceed the device's complement; polarised parts show polarity; unused inputs tied or marked.
- Reference designation: letters from 81346-2 (IEC), 315 clause 22 / Y14.44 (electronics) or ICS 19 (JIC); numbering gaps are warnings; one tag in two aspects only for the same object.
- Connection, wiring and terminal documents: every terminal entry matches a diagram connection and vice versa; every cable has both ends on a device; colour and notation agree with the 60445 / 60204-1 / NFPA 79 table in force; PE and N reserved.
- One-line: every bus has a voltage; transformers have kVA, voltages and vector group; OCPDs have frame and trip; relays have a valid C37.2 number and a CT or PT; sources carry fault current; device numbers unique per bus zone; panel schedule totals match the feeder.
- PLC I/O: each channel used at most once; every field terminal maps to a channel; I/O list, schematic tag and address agree; sinking/sourcing consistent per module; spares labelled.
- Logic diagram: no mixed shape sets per sheet; every input connected or marked; bubbles consistent with the declared logic convention.

## 7. The project document set and sheet frame

A machine-control set runs 15 to 30 pages with a fixed order: cover (project, machine, drawing number, page X of Y, revision index), index, power and one-line, control schematics, PLC I/O sheets, panel layout, terminal diagrams, cable and wire lists, bill of materials; building sets add plans, panel schedules and lighting ([industrialmonitordirect (weak)](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/electrical-cad-drawing-process-for-industrial-control-panels), [EC&M](https://www.ecmweb.com/design/article/20891591/proper-working-drawing-sets-an-engineers-perspective), [sheet index example](https://www.psdschools.org/sites/default/files/inline-files/EXHIBIT%20G%20%E2%80%93%20PHS%205.11.12%20UPDATED.pdf)). IEC 61082-1 refers sheet layout to ISO 5457 and title blocks to ISO 7200 (section 2). ASME Y14.1-2020 merged the inch and metric sheet standards: ANSI A through E with alternating aspect ratios versus ISO A4 to A0 at 1:root 2 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANSI/ASME_Y14.1)).

## Implications for a notation pack (synthesis)

This section is my synthesis, not sourced fact.

Pack family. One element taxonomy keyed by function (81346-2 classes as backbone: B sensor, F protection, K relay, M motor, P indicator, Q power switch, R passive, S operator control, T transformer, W conductor, X terminal) with five packs binding glyph sets, tag formats and sheet rules: Schematic IEC, Schematic ANSI/JIC ladder, One-line power, PLC I/O wiring, Logic diagram. Glyph style (IEC, ANSI, JIC) is a document-level variant on the same element types, switchable per project as in AutoCAD Electrical, with a validation forbidding mixed shape sets on a sheet.

Element types and ports. Contacts (NO, NC, changeover, timed, transition) with two ports and a parent-device link; coils (relay, contactor, solenoid, timer) with A1/A2; overload with heater ports and paired NC; pushbutton, selector, limit/pressure/level/flow/temperature switch; pilot light; fuse, breaker, disconnect, starter (composite); transformer with primary/secondary port groups and winding marks; motor, generator, utility, bus (junction with voltage), CT and PT (ratio, polarity), relay (device number); terminal (strip, number), connector, cable (conductor sub-ports), PLC module (channels as ports with address), junction, earth kinds, off-sheet reference pair; logic gate (shape set, input count, inversion per port), flip-flop, FBD block, SFC step, transition, action. Every glyph drawn in code from the public description.

Typed properties. Structured reference designation (function, location, product segments), terminal ids, wire number or potential, colour and cross-section, ratings, kVA and vector group, C37.2 number and suffix, CT/PT ratio, PLC address and I/O type, contact complement, DCC code, sheet metadata (project, drawing number, sheet, revision, grid scheme).

Identifier formats. IEC `=F1+A2-K3:13`; JIC `CR1`, `M1`, `OL1`, `PB1` with rung-derived wire numbers; electronics `R1`, `C1`, `U1`; power `52-1`, `87T`, `BUS-A 4.16kV`; PLC `I0.3` / `Q2.1` or vendor patterns; cross-references `/sheet.zone` with configurable separator, lettered rows skipping I and O, numbered columns.

Connection rules. Wire ports to wire ports only; power ports only within the one-line layer; contacts bind to coils through the parent link, never a wire; CT secondaries only to relays or meters; PE and N closed net classes; logic outputs to inputs, one driver per net; SFC step-transition alternation; LD left rail to contacts to coil to right rail, no reverse flow.

Validations. Error: duplicate designation, dangling off-sheet reference, contact without coil, coil complement exceeded, missing wire number, unresolved cross-reference, mixed shape sets, alternation break, invalid C37.2 number, duplicate PLC channel. Warning: numbering gaps, unused inputs, missing one-line ratings, missing polarity, missing DCC or title block fields, colour/notation mismatch. Info: coil not at right, more than one output per rung.

Licensing.
